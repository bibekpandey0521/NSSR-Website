Rural Outreach Program · By Students, For Students

Student Empowerment Initiative

A planned one-year science and digital learning program designed to help around 3,000 students in rural areas of Nepal learn, practice, and keep progressing beyond a single outreach visit.

Why SEI exists

The spark should not end when the team leaves.

The Student Empowerment Initiative responds to a practical gap in rural science education: students are curious, but many schools have limited laboratory access, few qualified science teachers, and very little opportunity to practice what they learn.

Over its planned one-year period, SEI will combine one-day hands-on launches at rural schools with a deeper pilot model. The proposed pilot includes projectors, a curated learning library, monthly near-peer mentorship, and a reference library across the 2026-27 academic year.

The two-layer model

Reach first. Stay connected.

01

One-day science launch

Students in Physics, Chemistry, and Biology take part in experiments, meet facilitators, and explore STEM pathways. Lab equipment and teaching materials are handed over to each host school for continued use.

02

Year-long pilot

One school receives a digital learning setup, a curated syllabus-matched video library, monthly teacher-guided mentorship, and books selected for Grades 9-12 or Grades 9-10 where no +2 program exists.

How the program works

Five practical supports

Hands-on launch

Experiments across Physics, Chemistry, and Biology, plus STEM career awareness.

Digital learning

Projectors, connectivity, and a shared learning library for ordinary lessons.

Near-peer mentorship

Monthly concept clinics led by NSSR student researchers and advisors.

Content library

Syllabus-aligned videos organized by topic for teachers and students.

Reference library

Guide books, model questions, past papers, and entrance preparation materials.

What success looks like

  • Every launch participant takes part in at least one experiment.
  • Host schools retain usable lab equipment and teaching materials.
  • A Grade 9-12 or 9-10 mentee cohort receives monthly academic support.
  • Term-test comparisons and short topic quizzes document learning progress.
  • The book and video libraries continue after the funding cycle.

Built to grow

Each component can stand alone or be combined into a complete program. This lets schools and partners begin with a launch event, then extend support to the digital pilot, mentorship, and library as resources allow.
